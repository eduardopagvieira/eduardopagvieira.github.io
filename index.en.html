About

I run the whole research. I define the question, collect and organize the information, analyze it and deliver a short report or a dashboard anyone in the company can read.

I hold a degree in social sciences and I'm doing a master's in political science at UFSCar. In practice, that makes me wary of the easy number. Before computing anything, I want to know what the indicator is supposed to measure and what the data leaves out.

I also spent years reading and coding text, from newspaper editorials to YouTube comments. Open-ended survey answers, customer reviews and social media comments are data to me like any other.

And I like explaining method. I have taught content analysis courses to researchers from many fields, always with hands-on examples.

How I use AI

I use AI to read more, not to think less. It expands the volume of text and data I can analyze, but it doesn't decide what they mean.

I research this topic with colleagues at INCT.DD, on the risks of using language models in qualitative research. The five rules I follow come from that work.

  1. I read first

    The model extends a coding I did by hand on part of the material. It doesn't start from scratch and doesn't replace reading.

  2. I check with people

    I validate on a sample read by humans, proportional to the dataset, and I state which sample it was.

  3. Agreement isn't enough

    I read the cases where the model and the readers disagree. That's where the errors a good average hides show up.

  4. Everything is logged

    I record the model, version and prompts used, so the result can be reproduced and audited.

  5. Scale, not speed

    I use AI when the volume calls for it. Creating the categories and interpreting the result stays with me.

In a company, this means reading thousands of comments, reviews or open-ended answers with the care of a human reading, and being able to tell the team where the model gets it wrong. I also prefer to apply AI to material that already exists, like texts and records, rather than putting it in place of the conversation with customers or employees.
